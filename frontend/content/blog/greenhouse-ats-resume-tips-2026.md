If you applied through Greenhouse and heard nothing, the problem often isn't your experience — it's that Greenhouse never built a clean profile from your file.

Greenhouse is popular with startups and mid-market tech. Recruiters love the workflow. Parsers still choke on the same resume mistakes that break [Workday](/blog/why-resume-rejected-by-workday) and Lever.

## What Greenhouse extracts first

Before a human opens your card, the system tries to pull:

1. **Contact fields** — email, phone, LinkedIn URL as plain text
2. **Work history** — company, title, dates, bullet text
3. **Education** — school, degree, year
4. **Skills / keywords** — overlap with the requisition

If any of those land empty or scrambled, you can still "submit successfully" and sit near the bottom of the ranked list. Success on the thank-you page ≠ success in the pipeline.

## Greenhouse-specific failure modes

**Two-column templates.** Design resumes look sharp in Preview. Greenhouse often reads the left column as noise or merges it with the wrong section. Keep one column: name → contact → summary → experience → education → skills.

**Contact info in the header/footer.** Many Word themes put email in the header. Humans see it. Parsers often skip headers. Put contact details in the body, directly under your name.

**Icons instead of labels.** A phone glyph is not a phone field. Write `Email: you@domain.com` in plain text.

**Tables and text boxes.** Skills grids and "skill bars" look modern and parse poorly. Use a simple comma-separated skills line or short bullets.

**PDF from Canva / Figma exports.** If the PDF is mostly vectors or images, extractable text is thin. Prefer Word or Google Docs → PDF, or DOCX if the posting allows it.

For the wider mechanics of scoring, see [how ATS resume screening works](/blog/how-ats-resume-screening-works-2026).

## Keyword placement that Greenhouse (and recruiters) both use

Greenhouse ranking still leans on [ATS keyword matching](/blog/ats-keyword-matching-complete-guide-2026). Do this in order:

1. Copy the job description into a side pane.
2. List every hard skill, tool, and role noun that appears more than once.
3. Put those phrases in **skills** *and* in **experience bullets** that describe real work.
4. Prefer the employer's exact wording over clever synonyms ("customer success" beats "client delight").

Stuffing a keyword block at the bottom is easy to spot and easy to ignore. Weave terms into outcomes you can defend in an interview.

## What recruiters see after the parse

Once you're past the filter, a recruiter skims:

- Title progression
- Company relevance
- Bullet clarity (impact, not duties)
- Gaps and date hygiene

So optimize for parse first, then for a 20-second human skim. Those goals don't conflict if you stay single-column and specific.

## Quick Greenhouse pre-apply checklist

- [ ] Single-column layout, standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills)
- [ ] Contact info in the document body, not only in header/footer
- [ ] No tables, icons-as-labels, or text-in-images
- [ ] JD hard skills appear in real bullets
- [ ] Dates consistent next to each role
- [ ] Re-scored against *this* posting, not last week's
