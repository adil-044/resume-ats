## Lever ATS Resume Tips  
The first dozen words you input can decide if literary genius gets dropped into the dumpster or flagged for an interview slot. Lever’s parser is a known snitch – it loves delimiters but hates anything unexpected. Below is the hard‑core guide you’ll use to turn Lever into your personal ally.  

### 1. Clean the Canvas – Start with a Plain Build  
Lever’s parser reads your “source” field, not the PDF viewer you see on your screen.  
* Use a single Word document or plain .txt file as your source.  
* Strip any background graphics, watermarks, or hidden images.  
* If you’re running macros, disable them – the parser will flag them as malicious.  

Keep the word count between 400–600 words. Over‑loading with detail muddles the signal Lever looks for.  

### 2. Master the Dictionary – Keyword Placement  
Lever’s algorithm scores by matching phrases to job‑specific taxonomy. The trick is to slot keywords where the parser’s ROI is highest:  
| Section | Parser Sensitivity | Placement Rule |
|---------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Header** | Medium | Put your job title and core skills in the first 75 characters. |
| **Objective** | Low | Optional; could be parsed as filler. Skip or keep one line. |
| **Work History** | High | Headline every role with a bold, standardized title (e.g., “Senior Marketing Analyst”). |
| **Skills** | Very High | Create a bulleted list right after work history, list each skill on its own line. |
| **Education** | Low | Keep format simple: “Degree, Institution, Year.” |

Don’t cram keywords into phrases; keep them distinct and separated by line breaks. This helps Lever associate them with services and reduces the chance of the skill list being buried in the parse.  

### 3. Handle the Quiet – Common Parse Errors  
| Error | Symptom | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Empty parse** | Lever shows “Resume didn’t parse.” | Convert to .docx, remove protected sections, and confirm the file is not corrupted. |
| **Mis"My title"** | Your real title appears as “Invited.” | Use standard title language; avoid descriptors like “Invited Project Lead.” |
| **Keyword bleed** | Skill names‑as‑full‑words mis‑identified (e.g., “Java” vs. “JavaScript”). | Place each skill on a new line; consider adding short clarifiers (Java (Script)). |
| **Formatting junk** | Resume parsed but shows “# …” or ASCII art. | Strip any tables, columns, or charts. Use plain lists. |

For a full checklist, see [/blog/resume-parse-errors-ats-fix-checklist].  

### 4. Trim the Noise – Formatting Tactics  
Lever’s parser hates arbitrary formatting:  

- **Avoid tables** – Convert them to plain lists.  
- **Plain bullets** – Use simple dashes or asterisks; the parser can’t read fancy Unicode bullets.  
- **No images** – Photos or icons trigger a false positive; keep your document image‑free.  

Remember, leveraging minimalism here speeds parsing and keeps key data intact.  

### 5. Sprinkle with Semantic Precision  
Lever aligns against the job’s *tags*.  
1. Review the job description.  
2. Highlight all role‑specific terms: “Agile,” “JSON APIs,” “UX Research.”  
3. Place these exact terms in your skills list and, if relevant, in bullet points under each role.  

If a skill appears as “Useroubt.io,” Renaming to “User experience design” eliminates ambiguity.  

### 6. Avoid the Ghosting Zone – Timing & Updating  
Lever keeps a cloud of resumes ready for each vendor. If your resume remains static for months, it gets deprioritized.  

- **Update quarterly**: Re‑upload a version that includes new projects or certifications.  
- **Add PDF metadata**: Include keywords in the PDF’s “Document Properties” – Lever reads those as part of the source.  

These moves double your visibility in the stack of resumes compiled by Recruiters’ leverage dashboards.  

### 7. Real‑World Example: The “Sales Collaboration” Role  
Your target job: “Senior Sales Engineer – Collaboration Platforms.”  

| Your Resume Section | Lever‑Friendly Update | Comment |
|---------------------|-----------------------|---------|
| Title: “Business Development Lead” | “Senior Sales Engineer” | Mimic title string to match taxonomy. |
| Skill: “Salesforce” | “Salesforce CRM” | Canonical term in Lever libs. |
| Project: “Launched email‑automation” | “Built (Python) • EmailAutomation • Increased sales by 12%” | Separate tools, languages, metrics. |
| Education: “B.S., Business” | “B.S. in Business Administration, State University, 2018” | Full descriptor. |

Loaded with granular tags, itline’s the best chance of appearing in the next pipeline run.
