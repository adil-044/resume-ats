Most rejections never reach a recruiter. They die in software.

## What an ATS is (and isn't)

An **applicant tracking system** ingests your resume, parses sections, and ranks you against the job posting. Popular stacks — Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo — differ in UI, but share the same failure modes: bad parse → missing keywords → low rank → never human-reviewed.

## What actually gets scored

1. **Structure** — Can the parser find experience, education, and skills as clean fields?
2. **Keyword overlap** — Exact phrases from the posting, not vague synonyms alone.
3. **Role relevance** — Titles and bullets that map to the requisition.
4. **Format hygiene** — Single-column text beats multi-column creative layouts.

Graphics, text-in-images, and unusual fonts still break parsers in 2026. Fancy ≠ readable.

## Why strong candidates still fail

You wrote for a hiring manager. The first reader is a tokenizer. "Grew revenue with clients" loses to "managed **enterprise account management** pipeline" when the JD says *account management*.

## Fix the filter, then the narrative

1. Paste the job description next to your resume.
2. List every hard skill and tool named in the JD.
3. Mirror vocabulary in real bullets — never fabricate.
4. Re-export as clean, single-column PDF or DOCX.
