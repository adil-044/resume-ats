Keyword matching is still the primary gate. Soft skills fluff rarely moves the rank. Exact tools and domain phrases do.

## Pull keywords from the posting

Highlight:

- Hard skills and tools (Python, Salesforce, GAAP, Kubernetes)
- Role nouns (incident response, demand planning, outbound SDR)
- Certifications and compliance terms
- Soft skills **only** when the JD repeats them as requirements

Ignore decorative adjectives. "Passionate self-starter" is noise.

## Place them where parsers look

| Location | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Skills section | Easy extract for filters |
| Experience bullets | Proves you used the skill |
| Summary | Sets topical relevance early |

Do **not** dump a keyword graveyard at the bottom. That pattern is easy to spot and easy to ignore.

## Synonyms vs exact match

Use the employer's wording first. Then one natural synonym if space allows. If they say "customer success," don't only write "client happiness."

## Measure before you apply

Score the resume against the JD. If match rate is low, rewrite bullets — don't spam the skills list. Tools like [HireReady](/#analyzer) show missing terms and help weave them into real experience.
